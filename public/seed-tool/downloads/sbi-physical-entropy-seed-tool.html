The one rule this tool is built on: a computer must never be the source of your seed’s randomness. Software randomness can be weakened or backdoored — by a malicious developer, a compromised supply chain, or AI-generated code no human fully reviewed — and you cannot tell by looking at the output. Physical randomness (coins, casino dice) that you produce cannot be predicted by anyone. This page only does the deterministic bookkeeping defined by BIP-39: the same flips in, the same words out, on any correct implementation, forever. That is what makes it auditable — and it is why you should verify the result independently before trusting it.
Before generating a seed for real funds:
  1. Verify this file’s SHA-256 hash against the hash published by the Scientific Bitcoin Institute.
  2. Move the file to a computer that is offline (best: a live-USB Linux session with Wi-Fi disabled, no disk).
  3. Work in private: no cameras, no smart speakers, no screen sharing. Write the words on paper only.
  4. When done on a live-USB session, power off; nothing persists. On a normal computer, treat the machine as having seen your seed.
  5. Never type a funded seed phrase into any internet-connected device, website, or app — no exceptions.

2 · Enter your physical entropy

Flip a coin and record each result: H or 1 for heads, T or 0 for tails. Spaces and line breaks are ignored. Vary your flipping technique; don’t reject “ugly-looking” sequences — real randomness contains long runs.

Advanced: von Neumann debiasing (for a possibly unfair coin)

Flips are taken in non-overlapping pairs: HT → 1, TH → 0, HH/TT discarded. This provably removes any constant bias of the coin, at the cost of needing roughly 4× more flips. It does not fix correlated flips — vary your technique regardless.

Advanced: mix a second independent source (XOR)

Enter a second, independently produced series (a different coin, a different person, a different day). The two series are combined bit-by-bit with XOR: the result is at least as random as the better of the two sources, so neither source needs to be trusted alone. Debiasing, if enabled, is applied to each series separately.

Why not let a computer pick the numbers?

Every wallet must turn 128–256 random bits into your keys. If those bits come from software, you are trusting an invisible pipeline: the RNG code, the compiler, the operating system, the hardware, and everyone who ever touched them. History includes real failures and real thefts from weak or rigged RNGs (Android’s 2013 SecureRandom flaw, the Trust Wallet browser-extension RNG flaw of 2023, the “Milk Sad” weak-entropy incident of 2023, among others). A modern concern is scale: code is increasingly written with AI assistance, and a subtly weakened RNG is exactly the kind of bug that survives casual review — its output still looks perfectly random.

Physical entropy inverts the trust: a coin or die in your hand cannot be influenced by any software, and the arithmetic that turns your flips into words is fully checkable. The computer is demoted from trusted oracle to auditable calculator — and for the truly paranoid, the calculation itself can be done entirely by hand.

Fully manual method (no computer at all)

As described in the Estudio Bitcoin article that inspired this page [1], the entire conversion can be done with pen and paper (a printable worksheet and numbered word list are included in the downloadable kit):

  1. Flip 128 (or 256) coin flips; write heads as 1, tails as 0.
  2. Group the bits into 11-bit chunks (the last chunk will be short by 4 or 8 bits — that’s the checksum’s slot).
  3. Convert each 11-bit chunk to a number 0–2047 (place values 1024, 512, 256, 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1) and add 1 to get the word number.
  4. Look up each word number in a printed BIP-39 word list.
  5. The final word requires a SHA-256 checksum, which is impractical by hand. Compute only this step on an air-gapped device (a hardware wallet with a “final word” feature such as SeedSigner, SpecterDIY or Coldcard) — or use this page offline and check that words 1–11 (or 1–23) match what you derived by hand.

This page is the calculator-assisted version of exactly that procedure, plus the dice option and self-tests.

Methodology, in full

1. The entropy target

A BIP-39 seed phrase [2] encodes a random number: 128 bits for 12 words, 256 bits for 24 words. Security rests entirely on those bits being unpredictable. Everything else — words, keys, addresses — is deterministic arithmetic derived from them.

2. Coin method (the Estudio Bitcoin procedure [1])

Each flip of a fair coin yields exactly one bit of entropy. Your flips, in the order entered (heads = 1, tails = 0), are the entropy — no transformation at all. This is the most transparent possible path: the 128 or 256 bits you wrote down with your own hand appear verbatim as the “entropy (hex)” in the audit trail, and the first 11-bit group is literally your first eleven flips.

3. Dice method (Coldcard convention [9])

A fair six-sided die yields log₂6 ≈ 2.585 bits per roll, hence the minimums: ⌈128 / 2.585⌉ = 50 rolls for 12 words, ⌈256 / 2.585⌉ = 99 rolls for 24 words. The roll string (ASCII digits 1–6) is hashed once with SHA-256 and the first 128 or 256 bits of the digest become the entropy. Hashing is used instead of naive base-6→base-2 conversion for three reasons: (a) it conditions the input — any residual bias or imperfection of the dice is diffused, and the output retains the full min-entropy of the rolls up to the digest size; (b) it avoids the modulo-bias pitfalls of ad-hoc base conversion; and (c) it exactly matches the convention documented and implemented by Coldcard [9], so the same rolls entered into a Coldcard, or piped through sha256sum, must produce the same seed — a free, independent, hardware cross-check. Notably, seeds generated this way with 50+ dice rolls were explicitly unaffected by Coldcard’s 2026 RNG failure [6], because the dice entropy never depended on the device’s RNG.

4. Optional: von Neumann debiasing (coin)

If a coin has constant bias p ≠ ½, raw flips carry less than one bit each. Von Neumann’s 1951 procedure [7] takes flips in non-overlapping pairs: since P(HT) = p(1−p) = P(TH), mapping HT → 1 and TH → 0 and discarding HH/TT yields provably unbiased bits from any constant bias, at an expected cost of 1 output bit per 1/(2p(1−p)) ≥ 2 pairs. It does not repair correlated flips — so vary your flipping technique regardless.

5. Optional: XOR mixing of two sources

The XOR of two independent bit streams is at least as unpredictable as the more unpredictable of the two. Producing two series (different coin, different person, different day) and combining them bit-by-bit means an attacker must compromise both sources; neither needs to be trusted alone.

6. The checksum word

BIP-39 appends ENT/32 checksum bits (4 for 128, 8 for 256) taken from the front of SHA-256(entropy), then splits the ENT+CS bits into 11-bit groups, each indexing one of 2¹¹ = 2048 words. This is why the last word is computed, not chosen — and why a hand-derived phrase needs one machine step (or a “final word” feature on an air-gapped signer [10]) to finish.

7. Why determinism is the security feature

Every step above is a pure function: same input, same output, on any correct implementation, forever. That is what makes the tool auditable — you verify it by agreement: this page, the companion Python app, the reference mnemonic library [3], Ian Coleman’s tool [8], a Coldcard, and pen-and-paper must all produce identical words from identical flips. A backdoor would have to hide inside deterministic, cross-checked arithmetic — where it cannot survive a single independent comparison. The on-load self-test runs the official BIP-39 test vectors [4] plus a dice vector and the word-list hash, and disables the tool on any mismatch.

Why this matters: a short history of entropy failures

Every incident below shipped seeds or keys that looked perfectly random. The flaw was always upstream, in the randomness pipeline — exactly the part physical entropy removes.

*At an implausibly generous 10¹² seed-guesses per second (a large GPU farm credited with zero derivation cost). Even repurposing the entire Bitcoin mining network (~10²¹ hashes/s) as a seed-cracker, exhausting 128 bits takes on the order of the age of the universe.

Where AI fits: brute force is bounded by compute and energy, not intelligence — a fair coin’s output contains no structure for a model to learn, so AI removes exactly zero bits from full physical entropy. What AI does accelerate is everything around the exponent: guessing human-chosen phrases, industrializing the scan for weak-RNG victims (the 2026 Coldcard sweep was offline precomputation plus blockchain matching), and slipping subtle RNG bugs into code. The defense is to make effective entropy equal theoretical entropy — which is the entire purpose of this method.
