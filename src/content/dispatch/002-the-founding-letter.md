**The Institute in one paragraph.** The Scientific Bitcoin Institute is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit dedicated to the *scientific* study of Bitcoin. That means treating Bitcoin the way physicists treat any interesting phenomenon: with models, tests, precise language, and the willingness to be wrong. Most Bitcoin coverage today is either promotional (from people who benefit if the price goes up) or dismissive (from people who don't understand it). Both miss the actual question: *what does Bitcoin look like when we measure it seriously?*

---

**What today looks like, measured seriously.**

As of this letter, Bitcoin is at approximately $63,000, which is **−1.09σ** below the long-run power-law trend for its price history. That's genuinely low — comparable to the depth of the 2022 cycle low. It sits well above the statistical floor at approximately $49,800 and nowhere near the falsification level of $16,700, at which the power-law hypothesis would need to be reconsidered.

![Bitcoin Power-Law Corridor — 20 July 2026](images/power-law-corridor-2026-07-20.png)

The wider market is in the middle of a familiar cycle debate. Bernstein maintains a $150K year-end target. Citigroup cut theirs from $112K to $82K. Standard Chartered holds at $100K. Prediction markets give 68% odds of $65K by end of July, but only 20% odds of $90K by year-end. ETF outflows continue — the funds bled $4.5B in June, their worst month since launch — and the Fed meets July 28-29 with markets pricing a 70% chance of a hold.

None of this changes the underlying model. When Bitcoin is at −1σ from a 16-year power-law trend, that's the *normal* deep-cycle position. It has happened repeatedly (2015, 2019, 2020, 2022). Every one of those cycle lows sat closer to the falsification threshold than today's price. The model has never been falsified.

That's what the Institute publishes: not price predictions, but calibrated distance from a well-tested reference point.

**How to read the Corridor.**

This is our simplest indicator, but also our most important. The math is straightforward: fit a straight line to log(price) vs log(days since Bitcoin's genesis). The result is what physicists call a power-law relationship — the same class of relationship that describes how populations of cities scale, how brain neurons grow, and how volcanic eruptions distribute in size.

For 16 years, Bitcoin has fluctuated around this line with remarkable consistency. The corridor is defined by the statistical distribution of those fluctuations. When price is at −1σ from trend, that's the 16-year historical bottom band. When it's at +2σ, it's approaching the top of the corridor. When it approaches −3σ, the model would be facing genuine falsification pressure.

The model doesn't say "Bitcoin will go up." It says: "given 16 years of history, this is where the model expects Bitcoin to sit, and this is how far it typically deviates." That's a fundamentally different claim than what most Bitcoin analysis makes.
